# Themes

How to make a theme of your own. A theme sets a page's colours, faces and the shape of the parts.

## Where themes are

- Themes of your own are in `FLOE_THEMES_DIR` (default `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/floe/themes`, else `~/.config/floe/themes`), a directory each. A file right there is not read, and nothing says so: put a theme's files in its directory.
- A name is lower case letters, digits, `-` and `_`, starting with a letter or a digit. A name of floe's themes is refused: yours sit beside floe's, and never take their place.
- A directory in them may not be a symlink; a file may.

```sh
floe theme new calm                   # makes the smallest theme, calm: an empty palette.css to fill in
floe theme check                      # checks every theme of your own, and the parts their files need
floe theme show default palette.css   # floe's palette, which yours goes over
```

A mistake of one stops every page, as `floe template guide` tells (*Where templates are*): `floe theme check` lists them. While you write one, make it in another directory and move it in once it is checked, as `floe template guide` tells (*Make one in another directory*): set `FLOE_THEMES_DIR` to it and run `floe theme check`.

A theme's file of a part or a look (`<name>.css`) is CSS of that part, scoped as its `part.css` is: what `floe part guide` tells of a part's CSS (*The CSS layers and @scope*) holds for it too, and the rules below are a theme's own.

## A theme's files

A theme of your own is a directory of these files, each left out if not needed. `floe theme new <name>` makes one: an empty `palette.css`, to fill in. `floe theme list` lists the themes, and `floe theme show <name>` lists the files one reads, floe's too, and prints one named after (`floe theme show ink palette.css`):

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `theme.yaml` | `description:`, what the theme is, in a line of text, which `floe theme list` and `floe theme show` print (`description: 'Black on cream, flat primaries'`), and the management page's settings show under the theme's card as written. floe's own themes are described there in the page's language. Without it a theme has an empty description. It holds no other key. |
| `palette.css` | The variables of the palette (`floe template guide`, *The palette*), and those of names of its own, on `:scope`, which is the page's root (`:root` takes the same element). It goes over floe's `default` palette, so it writes only what it changes. |
| `base.css` | Rules for the page around the parts. They reach the page, not what a part holds nor a text's Markdown, under the template's `page.css` and the looks. Write the root as `:scope`, not `:root` or `html`, which are outside the scope, and what a part holds in the file of that part. Variables of the theme's own may go on its `:scope` here too. |
| `markdown.css` | The look of Markdown (quotes, headings), wherever it stands: on the page, in a text and in what the parts hold. Every part's, look's and template's CSS wins over it. Write rules for what a part draws none of, as a quote or a heading: one for `p` or `ul` reaches a part's own too. |
| `<name>.css` | The style of the part or look `<name>`, scoped as its `part.css` is: `panel.css` reaches every panel, `code.css` code, a diff and a terminal. |

- A file of another name is refused.
- Write a colour once, in `palette.css`, and take it with `var()` (`var(--accent)`) in the other files, so that the template's colours on `:scope` and the palette agree.
- Leave `--floe-row-pad`, the padding above and below a row, and `--floe-pad-block` and `--floe-pad-inline`, a box's padding and a row's at the sides, as floe's base sets them: the rows and boxes of every part are padded alike in every theme. A theme pads no box: a frame it draws around a box (default's grey) is its own, and the element it paints inside reads the two variables.
- Leave `--floe-text-small` and `--floe-text-aside`, the two steps of parts' small text, as floe's base sets them. A size a theme gives a part's text of its own (a heading of a table drawn larger, a pill of no ground) is written as it is, in the part's file.
- A row's mark (note's, numbered's ring, timeline's dot, links' sign, the letters of `marked`, which `checklist` takes) is sized, its frame within, by `--floe-mark` and kept from the text by `--floe-mark-gap`; a theme sets those, not the mark's `top`, `left` or the row's padding, and hides the mark by `content: none`, its column then taking no room. The number of a card in cards keeps the same terms: sized by `--floe-mark` (set on `:scope.numbered`), hidden by `content: none` on `.numbered > .card::before`, and drawn as plain text by `--floe-mark: auto` with no background.
- A theme sets a part's variable on the `:scope` of the file of a part or look that sets or reads it: a look's file reaches every part that takes the look (`marked.css` reaches `checklist` and `decision`), and a part's file that part alone. A variable a part sets on an element in it (chart's `--c`, code's `--digits`) is its own, which a theme does not set. A theme may set `--checks-gap`, the room between what an item of `checks` holds, and `--entries-row-gap`, the room between the lines of an entry's title and tags.
- A card (`.card` in a part that takes cards) takes the panel too: a theme's `panel.css` draws it, and its `cards.css` writes what differs (the ground, the frame, the number), not a card's padding, which is the two variables'. The room between what a card holds is `--cards-gap` (0.4rem): a theme changes it by that variable, set on the `:scope` of its `cards.css`, and writes no `gap` on a card, which options holding parts, whose rows have no gap, lose without a word. Its first card is padded as the others: a theme lines it up with the column by no padding of 0.
- A theme draws upright and writes no word, as CSS text passes no `words.yaml` and stays in one language whatever the page's. A transform (`transform`, `-webkit-transform`, `-moz-transform`) moves alone, by `translate()`, `translateX()` or `translateY()`, or is `none`; a theme writes no `rotate`, `scale`, `zoom`, `offset-path`, `offset-rotate` or `perspective`, and no variable holding a turn. Its strings are marks alone, with a letter only in a typeface's name (`font-family`, `font`, `--font`, `--display`, `--mono`), written as themselves (no escape); `content` shows no `attr()`; a counter or list style (`counter()`, `counters()`, `list-style`, `list-style-type`) is `decimal`, `decimal-leading-zero` or a shape (`none`, `disc`, `circle`, `square`), by no variable, and a theme writes no `@counter-style`.
- A theme's decoration takes no room: draw it over the padding there is (a ground, `--floe-inset`, a `::before` laid over), not by widening the padding or by a frame of the ground's colour or none, so that it never vies with a part's padding. A frame drawn thicker is the frame's own shape (brutal's, editorial's top rule, washi's double rules), not room for a decoration. Draw no decoration by `outline` on a box where a part draws its focus ring or a pick by `outline` (options' cards): the ring takes the outline's place and the decoration is lost. Draw it by `--floe-inset` or `::before`, as washi's inner rule on a card is.
- A theme whose faces let what is under them through (aurora's glass) makes them opaque in a band, in its `band.css`, as aurora does: the band is filled with the accent, which would show through them and sink their text.
- A theme names its `@keyframes` and `@font-face` with its own name (`washi-fade`): those names are the page's, and the same name in a template's `page.css` wins over the theme's `base.css` and `markdown.css` (both in `floe.theme`), while a theme's file of a part wins over them all.
- A theme draws a tone from `var(--tone)` and `var(--tone-soft)` too, with no rule for each tone. A theme that fills the tones in colours of its own (brutal's, notebook's) names them once in its `palette.css`, as `--good-fill` and the like (`--accent-fill` for what has no tone), and draws with `var(--tone-fill)`, which floe sets from `--<tone>-fill`, or else `--<tone>-soft` for a tone it does not name. A theme's file of a part goes after the part's `part.css`, in the layer of the same depth: as specific, it wins, and a more specific selector wins by that.
- A table's head, the tab picked and code's heading are painted by their parts from variables, each read with floe's own value when unset, which a theme sets on the `:scope` of its file of the part (or of its `base.css`, for the whole page): a table's head by `--table-head-bg` and `--table-head-fg` (its ground painted on the head's row, so that a gradient runs once across it; a head of a gradient or of layers of background, placed and sized, cannot be a face, `--face`), its rule between rows by `--table-rule` (`none` for none); the tab picked by `--tabs-picked-bg`, `--tabs-picked-fg` and `--tabs-picked-ring` (a `box-shadow`); code's heading (a diff's and a terminal's too) by `--code-head-bg` and `--code-head-fg`, a diff's counts on it by `--code-head-add` and `--code-head-del`, and the copy button's ground by `--code-ground` (code's lines are on the code's own ground: a theme that paints them a ground of its own, on `:scope pre`, sets the same colour in `--code-ground`). A theme writes no rule for a state (`:checked`) or for print, nor colours a part's heading anew for another part: one that lightens code's heading sets the counts' colours with it. A terminal's heading is fainter, at 85% of `--code-head-fg`, whatever colour a theme gives it; a theme that writes the heading's `color` by a rule draws a terminal's at that colour. The picked tab's ring is drawn over a shadow a theme gives every tab title: a theme that wants that shadow on the picked one too sets it as the ring. A heading's rule and its type (face, size, weight) are rules of the theme's, on `:scope thead th` or `:scope figcaption`; a head a theme fills with a ground of its own is a face (`--face`, `--on-face`).
- A quiet ground within a box (a table's head, a bar's track, a keycap) is `var(--quiet)`. A theme that paints a box in a ground near it sets `--quiet` on that box (default sets the surface on its grey panels, and its grey on the faces of the surface within them).
- A chart's shapes are variables too, each read with floe's own value when unset, which a theme sets on the `:scope` of its `chart.css`, taking no room: `--chart-line` and `--chart-dash` (a line's width and dashes), `--chart-dot`, `--chart-dot-fill`, `--chart-dot-edge` and `--chart-dot-line` (a dot's radius, fill, edge and the edge's width), `--chart-bar-fill`, `--chart-bar-edge` and `--chart-bar-line` (a bar's fill, frame and the frame's width), and `--chart-ground` (the ground under the plot, for a box with a pattern that would run into its ticks). `currentColor` is the series' colour. Keep a line to 3px, a dot's radius to 5px, its edge and a bar's frame to 2px, which the labels are kept clear of. A bar too thin for its frame draws without one.
- Round corners are the palette's: a theme sets `--radius`, `--radius-small` and `--radius-pill` in `palette.css`, one length each, and its other files leave the corners to them. Corners of other shapes, such as notebook's drawn by hand, are variables of the theme's own in its `palette.css` or on the `:scope` of its `base.css`, read in its files: one of the three holding four corners or a `/` voids a rule that reads it as one corner or in `calc()` or `min()`. A corner may take the smaller of two variables (`min(var(--radius-pill), var(--floe-pill-end))`, a pill's).
- A shadow is the theme's one, given once in `palette.css` as `--floe-shadow-x`, `-y`, `-blur` and `-spread` (in px or rem: one in em grows with a box's font, and a percentage leaves no shadow).
- A theme writes no room between parts: it is `--floe-room` everywhere, set by the parts and templates, and a gap on the root of a theme's `cards.css` or `tabs.css` moves it.
- A data file picks the theme with `theme:`, as one of floe's.

## Checking

`floe theme check` does what `floe part check` does: a theme's `<name>.css` needs its part, so parts and themes are checked together. It prints every mistake of them (exit code 1): of a theme's files, its name and its CSS, and those of the parts, looks and shared schemas of your own.
